# Viral Content Rules

All 41 rules below are mandatory and form the viral-specific layer on top of `writer`'s clean-prose engine. Apply every rule when drafting the body.

## VIRAL CONTENT RULES (all mandatory — viral-specific layer)

0. Start from people's resentment and show perspective
1. Don't separate the first sentence from the paragraph — write as one block
2. NO bold formatting
3. Only numbered lists (1., 2., 3.) when points are needed
4. Lots of details and specifics — substance, not fluff
5. Value worth saving and forwarding
6. Controversial, polarizing takes from real pain points
7. To the point with details: examples, quotes in guillemets, numbers
8. "Magic pill" effect — easy to implement
9. Only insights and WOW — nothing obvious
10. No value judgments — let the reader judge
11. Micro-conclusion with NLP question after the last point
12. Strictly respect the character limit
13. Conversational language, not business correspondence
14. Short paragraphs — you're not Tolstoy
15. Never write "Micro-conclusion:" explicitly
16. NEVER insert asterisks or other symbols inside words
17. Quotes and real examples for clarity
18. Address different psychological vectors
19. Don't start paragraphs with questions
20. NLP question after the last point, before CTA
21. EVERY paragraph is unique in form and content (dopamine hit). No repeated templates. If one has a quote — another CANNOT. If one has a story — another CANNOT. If one uses form X — another must use form Y.
22. Don't label parts ("Example:", "Quote:")
23. No "One man said:" — use specific characters
24. Don't end a paragraph with a generalization
25. Every paragraph engages a vivid emotion
26. Makes you want to repost to show off your views
27. Emphasize emotions and states for engagement
28. Hit real socio-cultural context
29. "WOW" effect: "what the f***? I didn't know that!"
30. Start immediately with points (or text) — NOTHING before them, no titles!
31. Each point: 4-6 sentences, minimum 50 words
32. Storytelling runs through the entire piece
33. Only guillemet quotes for Russian, straight quotes for English (enforced by writer)
34. All points with double spacing as paragraphs
35. DETAILS = visual anchors: quotes, addresses, numbers, examples, real situations — so the text doesn't read as a wall, the eye catches on "berries", maximum value per unit of text
36. Reveal mechanisms deeply, not surface-level descriptions
37. In EVERY point — polarity, provocation or controversy; no neutral statements
38. Zero fluff, slogans, abstractions, or filler sentences; don't pad the volume
39. Don't end a paragraph with an abstraction or question
40. Every point — an insight with "wow" effect that flips perspective; nothing obvious or common knowledge
41. When making edits, implement changes WITHOUT breaking other rules

## EN viral hook patterns

EN-language layer for Twitter/X, LinkedIn, and Substack viral content. Applies on top of the 41 rules above. Where a viral rule above is language-agnostic (numbered list discipline, no labels, no bold), it applies as written. The patterns below replace the RU-specific guidance with EN-specific equivalents.

### Hook openers that work (EN)

Lead the piece with one of these constructions. Each presupposes the reader is about to learn something only-an-insider would know.

- "Most people don't know..."
- "Here's what nobody tells you about X..."
- "I spent 5 years figuring out X. Here's the shortcut."
- "The biggest mistake in X is..."
- "Stop doing X. Start doing Y."
- "Everyone tells you to X. They're wrong because..."
- "I was wrong about X for years. Here's what changed my mind."
- "If you're trying to X, read this before you start."

Rule: every opener must promise a specific payoff inside the first sentence. No throat-clearing.

### Counter-intuitive claim hooks

The hook lands harder when it inverts conventional wisdom. Pattern:

- "Conventional wisdom: X. What actually works: Y."
- "Everyone says X. The data says Y."
- "I used to believe X. Then I tried Y."

Rule: the inversion must be defensible by the body of the piece. Empty contrarianism (inverting just for the hook) breaks rule 40 (genuine insight, not surface).

### Specificity beats generality

Always use a concrete number, name, or instance over an abstraction. Same as RU rule 35 (visual anchors).

- BAD: "high ROI"
- GOOD: "3.7x ROI in 90 days"
- BAD: "many customers"
- GOOD: "127 paid customers in the first month"
- BAD: "famous founder"
- GOOD: "Stripe's Patrick Collison"

Rule: every claim in the body must carry a number, name, quote, or specific situation. No abstract claims survive.

### Banned hook formats (EN)

These read as machine or as low-effort filler. Hard ban.

- "In a world where..." — cliché movie-trailer opener.
- "Have you ever wondered..." — passive, low-tension, AI-style.
- "Let's dive in." — no value delivered, just stalling.
- "Buckle up." — TikTok-style filler.
- "Imagine if..." — pure setup, no payoff in the line itself.
- "I'll let you in on a secret..." — coy, low-status framing.

### EN numbered-list discipline

Inherits global rule 3 (only numbered lists for points). EN-specific tightening:

- Lead each item with a verb, not a noun. "Stop optimizing for X" > "The avoidance of X".
- ≤7 words per item line; if a point needs unpacking, it goes in the paragraph under the item, not in the item line itself.
- No closing punctuation on the item title line — keeps the eye moving down the list.

### EN micro-conclusion (NLP question)

Inherits rules 11 and 20 (NLP question after the last point, before CTA). EN-specific framings that work:

- "What would change in your work if you started today?"
- "If you knew this 5 years ago — where would you be now?"
- "Which of these are you actually doing this week?"
- "What's the one thing on this list you're avoiding?"

Rule: the question must be answerable in the reader's head in under 3 seconds. No rhetorical sprawl. No "Have you ever thought about..." (banned opener).

### EN CTA patterns

- "Repost if this saved you an hour."
- "Follow for more like this — one a day."
- "DM me 'X' for the full breakdown."
- "Sub to my newsletter — link in bio."

Rule: single verb, single ask, no stacking. Two CTAs = zero conversions.

### EN typography overrides

Inherits writer typography for EN (straight quotes, hyphen `-` with spaces or em-dash sparingly). Specific to viral context:

- No em-dash overuse — viral text reads on mobile; em-dash density above 1-per-paragraph reads as AI.
- Use straight quotes `"X"`, not curly `"X"` (curly quotes inconsistently render on X/Twitter).
- Numbers as digits, always: "3 ways", not "three ways".
