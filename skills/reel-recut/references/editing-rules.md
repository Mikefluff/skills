# Editing rules

What decides the plan. The builder enforces the mechanical half (where a cut may land, how long a framing is held); this file is the half that is judgement.

## Takes

- Transcribe everything first. Two clips are compared by their text, never by their file names or thumbnails.
- Among duplicates keep the take that finishes the thought, then the higher resolution, then the cleaner delivery.
- A short take that is the first half of a longer one adds nothing. Drop it.
- A line said twice in a row is a restart the camera kept: cut from the first start to the second.
- A clip that stops mid-sentence is still usable when the missing part is one plain statement. Close it with a `tail`. When the missing part is an argument, tell the user it needs a re-shoot.
- Trim connective openers ("next topic", "in this episode"). A reel starts on its subject.

## The first three seconds

- The hook is two lines: the topic in the speaker's words, then the tension or the question.
- The first graphic or cutaway lands inside the first four seconds. A viewer who sees a static face for five seconds has already left.
- Nothing covers the face in the first second.

## Graphics

- A graphic names what is being said in that second, in fewer words: four to six, never a sentence.
- One idea on screen at a time. A pill leaves before the next arrives, except in `vs`, which is the comparison itself.
- Numbers, amounts and durations get `big`. A contrast gets `vs`. Three or more spoken items get a `panel`, with each row timed to the word that names it.
- `warn` is for a risk and `ok` for a confirmed fact. When every pill is a warning, none is.
- An address is shown with `url` only when the user confirmed it exists and says what the caption claims.
- Do not put on screen anything the speaker did not say. Tightening the wording is fine; adding a claim is not.

## Cutaways

- Two or three per reel, two to three seconds each. More, and the reel stops being a person talking.
- Each one illustrates the line it covers: a counter for "at the office", a calculator for "the price".
- Never over the hook, never over the last line before the end card.
- Prompts ask for no readable text, no logos, and no flags unless a specific flag is the point. Check each generated asset before it goes in the plan: an invented flag or a garbled document is common.
- Documents, cards and passports in generated footage are props. Faces out of frame.

## Captions

- Read every caption in `check` output. Names, abbreviations and foreign words are where Whisper goes wrong; fix them with `word_fix`.
- An abbreviation heard as two words is fixed by replacing the first and dropping the second.

## Covers

- A frame with open eyes, facing the camera. Scan a few seconds either side of the first choice.
- Three short lines; one of them highlighted. The title is the hook, shortened.
- All covers of a series share the layout and carry the number.

## Review

Open the contact sheet of every reel and check:

1. The hook is readable and gone by the fourth second.
2. No caption sits on the face; under a panel the caption moves up with the speaker.
3. Every graphic in the plan appears, and none overlaps another.
4. Every cutaway shows what its line says, with no stray flag or text.
5. The framing changes; no stretch looks frozen.
6. The tail, when there is one, shows its cutaway and list, not a frozen speaker.
7. The end card has the wordmark and every button.

Then open every cover. What the sheet cannot show is the sound: say so in the report and ask the user to listen to two reels.
