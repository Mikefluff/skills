# Length budget

The recipient should be able to read the email in ≤15 seconds. That means:

- **Target**: ≤120 words for first-touch
- **Hard cap**: 200 words
- **Follow-up**: ≤80 words
- **Intro-request (the forwardable)**: ≤80 words
- **Re-engage**: ≤100 words

---

## Why these numbers

15 seconds at average reading speed (≈250 words/min) = ~62 words. Plus subject + signature ≈ 100 words. The 120-word target gives one buffer sentence; 200 is the point where the recipient starts skimming and the email becomes a TL;DR exercise.

Above 200 words, response rates drop sharply (anecdotal evidence + industry data both confirm). If you can't make the ask in 200 words, you don't yet have the ask clear in your own head — go back to the brief.

---

## When draft is over budget — the cut-list

Apply in order until under budget. The order matters: cheapest cuts first.

### Cut 1: ceremony

Every banned opener and closer. See `banned-patterns.md`. This alone often cuts 20-40 words.

### Cut 2: hedges

Replace "I was wondering if ..." → "Could we ..." Replace "It would be amazing if ..." → "Could we ..." Strip "just", "wanted to", "I was hoping". This is another 10-20 words.

### Cut 3: redundant qualifiers

- "as you may know" — delete
- "as we've discussed before" — delete (recipient knows what you discussed)
- "in my opinion" / "personally I think" — delete (they know it's your opinion)
- "obviously" / "clearly" — delete (if it's obvious, don't state it)
- "needless to say" — delete (literally needless)

### Cut 4: filler adjectives + adverbs

- "really excited" → "excited" (or delete the sentiment entirely)
- "incredibly impressive" → "impressive" (or pick a stronger noun)
- "very interesting" → "interesting" or specific reason
- "extremely useful" → "useful" or skip

### Cut 5: explanation of explanation

If a sentence explains the sentence before it, ask whether the prior sentence is doing its job. Often: rewrite the first sentence to be self-explanatory, delete the second.

Example:
> "We grew 4x in 6 months. By that I mean we went from $1M to $4M in ARR between January and July."

→

> "We grew from $1M to $4M ARR between January and July."

The second sentence absorbs the first. ~20 words → ~12.

### Cut 6: the second ask

Cold emails have exactly one ask. If your draft has two, pick the more important one and cut the other. (Or send two separate emails, with separate subjects.)

### Cut 7: bio that isn't load-bearing

If the recipient already knows who you are (you're being introduced, or they've seen you before), drop the bio paragraph. The signature carries enough.

If they don't know you, the bio must justify the next ~30 seconds of their attention. Two facts. Not five.

### Cut 8: links

You can link to ONE thing. Two only if absolutely necessary (e.g. the deck and the demo). Never three. Each link costs ~5 words of friction.

---

## When the draft is at budget but still feels long

It probably is. Try this final pass:

1. Read aloud. Anywhere you stumble — that's a cut.
2. Mentally remove each paragraph. Does the email still hold? If yes, delete that paragraph.
3. The "first sentence test": if you delete the first sentence, does the email still make sense? If yes, the first sentence wasn't pulling weight — find a better one or cut.

---

## What NOT to cut

- The specific proof (numbers, names, prior result)
- The specific ask (time-bounded if possible)
- The recipient's name in the salutation
- The sign-off + your name

These are load-bearing. Cut everything else first.

## Budget summary

| Email type | Target | Hard cap |
|---|---|---|
| First-touch | ≤120 | 200 |
| Follow-up | ≤80 | 120 |
| Intro-request (forwardable) | ≤80 | 120 |
| Intro-request (to intro-giver) | ≤60 | 100 |
| Re-engage | ≤100 | 150 |
| Warm-intro template (forwardable) | ≤80 | 120 |

Subject line is counted separately. Cap subject at 50 characters.
