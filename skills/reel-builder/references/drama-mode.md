# Drama mode — a series of dialogue films, from an idea to a checked episode

Drama mode makes long narrative video: episodes of 8-12 minutes, built from 15-second Seedance 2.5 scenes in which several characters speak in the series' language and Seedance voices them. Episodes of one series share a cast, places and a director, so the second episode costs one sentence of direction.

Everything runs in this repo. The agent that runs the skill writes the story and the prompts, Nano Banana Pro draws the faces and places, Seedance renders the scenes, and ElevenLabs Scribe with a Gemini critic checks the result. No web interface, no image service in a browser, no downloads by hand.

```
<series>/
  bible.json          SERIES call: language, look, cast, locations, episode outlines
  director.md         DIRECTOR call: rules that extend directors/base.md
  cast/<id>.png       reel_cast: one judged portrait per character
  cast/<id>.mp3       reel_cast: a 7 s voice per speaking character (ElevenLabs voice design)
  locations/<id>.png  reel_cast: one judged plate per place and state
  cast.json           what was rendered from which description (reuse across episodes)
  ep01/
    plan.json         EPISODE call, then reel_cast composes each scene's prompt
    shots/            reel: one clip per scene
    final.mp4         reel: the episode with its own dialogue (+ score, if planned)
    check.json        reel_check: speech and faces per scene
```

## The sequence

| Step | Run | Look at before going on | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Research (optional) | `research-brief` on what gets views in this genre and language: titles, hooks, lengths | the brief | — |
| 1. Series | ONE Agent with SERIES from [`drama-system-prompt.md`](../../../common/video-prompt-library/drama-system-prompt.md) → `bible.json` | cast, wants, episode events | — |
| 2. Director | ONE Agent with DIRECTOR + `directors/base.md` → `director.md` | 5-15 rules, each checkable on a shot | — |
| 3. Episode | ONE Agent with EPISODE + `drama.director("<series>/director.md")` → `ep01/plan.json` | beats, every line in its shot | — |
| 4. Cast | `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel_cast --plan-file <series>/ep01/plan.json` | `cast/`, `locations/`: faces right, weather right for its scenes | ≈ $0.14 per asset, one take; re-draw by eye with `--only` |
| 5. Price | `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel --plan-file <series>/ep01/plan.json --cost-only` | the total | — |
| 6. One scene | `reel --plan-file <series>/ep01/plan.json --only sc01 --yes` then `reel_check --only sc01` | the scene, and whether its speech passed | one scene |
| 7. Episode | `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel --plan-file <series>/ep01/plan.json --resume --yes` | `final.mp4` | see below |
| 8. Check | `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel_check --plan-file <series>/ep01/plan.json` | `check.json`; it prints the `--redo` line | ≈ $0.01 per scene |
| 9. Re-render | the printed `reel ... --redo sc03,sc07`, then `reel_check --only sc03,sc07` | until it prints nothing to redo | per scene |

Step 6 is the director's test: render one scene, watch it, and change the director's rules before 40 scenes are paid for. A rule changed after step 3 needs the EPISODE call again; a scene changed by hand needs only `reel_cast` (it re-composes prompts and renders nothing new).

The next episode: run EPISODE with the next outline and the previous episode's last two scenes, into `ep02/plan.json`, then steps 4-9. `reel_cast` reuses every face and place whose description has not changed.

## What it costs

Seedance 2.5 bills tokens: seconds × width × height × 24 / 1024, $10.70 per million on BytePlus and OpenRouter. One 15-second 16:9 scene is about $1.54 at 480p and $3.47 at 720p. A 10-minute episode, 40 scenes, is about $62 at 480p and $139 at 720p, before re-renders. Every scene `reel_check` sends back costs the same again. Run `--cost-only` for the exact figure; it reads each scene's own length and resolution.

## Speech: what is known, what is measured

What the sources say, as of 2026-10-15:

- **Languages.** BytePlus says only "10+ languages" ([product page](https://www.byteplus.com/en/product/modelark)), and its docs pages render no text for a fetcher. The eleven most often listed are Chinese, English, Spanish, Indonesian, Malay, Thai, Arabic, Portuguese, Vietnamese, Japanese and Korean ([OrcaRouter](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/seedance-2-5), which gives no source).
- **Per-language scores exist only for 2.0.** The [Seedance 2.0 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14148), Table 20, rates image-to-video on a 1-5 scale. Audio prompt following, meaning the model says the words it was asked for, scored English 4.20, Indonesian 4.14, Spanish 4.00, Portuguese 3.63, Korean 3.38 and Japanese 3.13. Thai, Arabic, Vietnamese and Malay were not scored. The report names "lip-sync errors in multi-speaker scenes" and audio distortion as open problems.
- **2.5 says the words far more accurately but holds a voice less closely.** In an independent benchmark ([ORAV](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34843v1), Table 3), 2.5 has 5.5 % word and character error rate against 16-19 % for 2.0. Its cosine similarity to a reference voice is 0.45, against 0.48 for 2.0 and 0.59 for MiniMax-H3. No language list or lip-sync score for 2.5 is published.
- **Voice references carry identity; they are not a copy.** In a two-speaker test, giving each speaker their own `@Audio` voice, about 7 s each so the total stayed under 15 s, mapped the voices correctly to two named speakers. Without an instruction, the sample's room and microphone carried into the scene ([Genflick](https://genflick.com/blog/seedance-voice-consistency-audio-reference-2026-05-03)).
- **Two speakers in one shot** sometimes get each other's lines ([videoai.me](https://videoai.me/blog/seedance-2-0-dialogue-prompts)), so base rule 9 gives each line its own shot.

What the pipeline does with it:

- **A voice per character, once per series.** For a character with `voice_text`, ElevenLabs designs a voice from its `voice` description, and the sample is cut to 7 s (`cast/<id>.mp3`). Every scene the character speaks in carries it as `@Audio`, with the line "use it only for timbre, accent and pace; do not copy its room". Given 2.5's weaker voice matching, this narrows the drift between scenes but does not remove it.
- **The 2.5 dialogue syntax.** `Dialogue language: Thai.` comes before the lines, then `Malai says (…): {…}` per line, sound effects in `<>`, and "No music".
- **The words are measured, not trusted.** `reel_check` transcribes the part of each scene the edit uses with ElevenLabs Scribe, in the bible's language, and compares it with the script letter by letter, because Thai, Japanese and Chinese write no spaces. A scene where less than 0.8 of the script is heard is re-rendered.
- **When the same scene fails twice, the line is the problem.** Shorten it (base rule 9), give it its own close-up, or move the second speaker's line to the next scene.
- **A language that fails on most scenes** is one Seedance does not voice well. Pick another language for the series, or voice it outside the scene with film mode: an ElevenLabs take, with Seedance lip-syncing to it.

On the OpenRouter route, voice references need a public URL. Either set `"media_host": "litterbox"` in the plan to allow uploading them for an hour, or render on `seedance-2-5` (BytePlus), which takes the files inline.

## Faces

- A scene sends its speakers' portraits and its place as references, and the runner writes the line that says which Image is whom. The chain never counts images.
- Seedance rejects a reference or keyframe with a photoreal face it takes for a real person (`InputImageSensitiveContentDetected.PrivacyInformation`), and the whole scene fails. A generated face is not exempt: a Nano Banana keyframe tripped it in the first film-mode trial. When a scene fails with it, re-roll that portrait with `reel_cast --only <id>`; a look with a light painterly or animated rendering avoids the gate entirely. Extras never show sharp faces (base rule 16).
- `reel_check` scores identity against the portraits and flags a character doubled in one frame or subtitles burnt in. Both are common Seedance failures.

## Score and sound

The scene's own sound is the dialogue and diegetic noise; base rule 13 keeps music out of it, so the score can be laid over without fighting a second one. A `music` block in the plan (as in an ordinary reel, `--music-provider eleven-music` for an instrumental) is mixed under the dialogue and ducked whenever someone speaks. With no music block the episode keeps exactly the sound Seedance made.

## The first-frame route: Wan 3.0, the cast's voices, one grade

When Seedance's filter refuses the faces, a trailer can be rendered on `openrouter-wan-3-0`: Wan 3.0 on OpenRouter, image-to-video from the storyboard frame alone, at 480p for about $0.05 a second. It took every generated face it was given (2026-10-16), but it accepts no references and no audio, so three things move out of the generation and into the cut:

- **Lens, angle and light live in the storyboard frame.** Wan keeps the first frame's lens and composition. An image model ignores "21 mm, horizon tilted 10°" and draws an ordinary 50 mm frame, so the board prompt describes what the lens does to the picture ("his face fills the left two-thirds and bulges toward the lens, the ceiling and its caged tube in frame, the whole frame rotated 15 degrees").
- **The motion prompt is camera first, then the performance.** A long style paragraph buries the move; Wan then barely moves. The RESTAGE call of [`drama-system-prompt.md`](../../../common/video-prompt-library/drama-system-prompt.md) writes both: a new prompt per board frame and a `motion` per shot. A top-down camera must be told to stay overhead, or Wan swings it to eye level.
- **The voice is the cast's, the mouth is Wan's.** Wan speaks the quoted line with its own voice; that voice is never used. `reel_animatic --clips` asks Scribe when the clip's mouth starts the line and trims the clip so that moment lands where the cast's ElevenLabs take plays. A take much longer than Wan's reading still drifts at the end of the word.
- **The look is one grade over every clip** (`"grade": "rocknrolla"`, `common/runners/film_grade.py`): generations colour each clip differently; the cut does not ask them to agree.

| Step | Run |
|---|---|
| Restage | ONE Agent with RESTAGE + the merged director rules → write `board.frames[].prompt`, `shots[].motion`, `"video_provider": "openrouter-wan-3-0"`, `"grade"` |
| Board | `reel_board --plan-file <plan> --only <changed frames> --yes` |
| Matrix | `reel_matrix --plan-file <plan> [--pin t26=seedance] --apply`: one row per shot (size, line, camera, route and why, transition in, takes and what they cost, which take the cut uses, next action) in `<out>/matrix.md`. A speaking shot or one carried by a face goes to Wan; a shot carried by its camera or action, or a device's view, to Seedance with its previs as Video 1. Pins survive rebuilds |
| Compose | `reel_cast --plan-file <plan>` (sends the frame and the motion, nothing else) |
| Render | `reel --plan-file <plan> --resume --skip-stitch --yes` |
| Cut | `reel_animatic --plan-file <plan> --clips --yes` → `trailer.mp4` |

The clip's own sound stays under the cast's take with its voice removed by contour, not by a hole in time: with `audio-separator` installed (on PATH or `$AUDIO_SEPARATOR`, model `vocals_mel_band_roformer.ckpt` in `~/.cache/audio-separator`), Mel-Band RoFormer splits each clip into voice and background and the cut keeps the background whole; without it the line's window is ducked.

A shot cut by hand (a CCTV composite, a screen replacement) carries `"clip": "shots/<file>.mp4"`; it is not rendered and the cut takes it as it is. Wan holds a face only from the first frame, so a face can drift over a long shot; keep speaking shots short.

## A short film from one picture

A film whose cast already exists in one picture (a group portrait, a poster) sets `"master": "master.png"` in the bible. Every portrait and plate `reel_cast` draws is sent the picture as Image 1, so faces, colours and proportions are copied, not invented, and a board frame may list `"master"` in its refs (a group shot, a final image that returns to the picture). The SHORT call of [`drama-system-prompt.md`](../../../common/video-prompt-library/drama-system-prompt.md) writes the whole film to its end, from the user's outline, in the trailer's plan shape with its soundtrack, into `<series>/short/plan.json`. From there it is the trailer's sequence: the story check, `reel_cast`, `reel_board`, `reel_animatic` (watch it), and only then `reel --cost-only` and the render.

**The story check comes before anything is drawn.** Spawn one Agent with [`story-check-prompt.md`](../../../common/video-prompt-library/story-check-prompt.md) as `system` and the screenplay as the message: `drama.screenplay_text(plan, bible)` renders each shot's action and lines in film order with the title cards, and the prompt file has the one-line command. The reader knows nothing but the screen and returns `{"problems": [{"beat", "missing", "fix_hint"}]}` for every object, rule or plan whose meaning, motive or payoff is not set up. Fix every problem in the plan before `reel_board`. In the Tanya short the glowing jar («блестяшка») was seen but never explained: nobody said what it was, that one drop was the rule, or why the babies poured in all of it, and four shots had to be shot after the cut. The reader flags that jar from the earlier plan.

**`reel_cast` sets the vendor-filter flags first** (`drama.preflight`, one note per flag on stderr). If a child speaks in a shot (in its dialogue or its `vocals`; the bible marks a child by `"age"` under 13 or by a child's word in `voice` or `identity`), or if the body has someone singing, the shot gets `kwargs.generate_audio: false`. Seedance refuses such audio with a copyright filter, and the cast's take replaces the generation's audio anyway. A shot whose previs camera is locked off is only noted: a still clay clip is sometimes read as a video to edit, but on the first film just one such shot in five was refused, so `"no_previs": true` is set by eye. A shot that already sets `generate_audio`, true or false, is left alone. `"no_keyframe"` stays a call made by eye after a refusal: a frame the filter takes for someone else's character cannot be seen in the plan.

**`reel_previs` checks its own framing.** After rendering, it projects each cast member's head through the shot's previs camera at the start, middle and end (`previs.framing`, pure Python, the same figure proportions as the Blender script). A shot is flagged when a head is behind the lens or off the frame, when a close shot puts the head's centre within half its radius of an edge (the face is cut, not just the hair), or when the head is smaller than the shot size in the body allows: 15% of the frame height for a close shot, 20% for a medium, 4% for a wide. A group of three or more may lose a third of its heads at the edges. The flags are printed and written to `previs/framing.json`, and nothing changes in the plan unless `--auto-skip` is passed, which sets `"no_previs": true` on the flagged shots so the storyboard keyframe leads instead. Seedance copies a previs literally: in the Tanya short the close-ups of the babies (height 0.5) framed their bellies (t08b), and a "medium two-shot" showed them small in a wide room (t08w). Both were taken off their previs by eye, and the check now flags both. It flagged 23 of the 83 previs shots, and the clips confirmed them. Read the flags before passing `--auto-skip`, because a deliberate crop gets flagged too.

**Coverage of an exchange.** For two or more people talking, the SHORT call writes a wide shot of everyone listening, with no line in it, then a close-up per line, then a shot that reuses the wide's tail (`"clip"`). Every shot of the exchange carries `"coverage": "<the wide's label>"`, and `drama.validate` checks that. The tail's `"clip"` reads `shots/<wide label>.mp4` until the wide is rendered. After that, put in the file name from `manifest.json`.

A scene added after the first cut goes in with `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel_insert --plan-file <series>/short/plan.json --from inserts.json --dry-run`, then again without `--dry-run`. Each entry of `inserts.json` places one shot with `"insert_after"`, `"insert_before"` or `"replace"` and a shot label, and brings the shot with its board frame, previs and vocals; a shot cut by hand from an existing take carries only `"clip"`, `"trim_start"` and `"trim_seconds"`. Everything after the insertion point moves by the new shot's length: shots, title cards, sound placements and the total. Shots are numbered again and the board frames move with them. The plan is checked against its bible and previs before it is written, the previous plan is kept as `plan.json.bak`, and the command prints which steps to run next for the new labels only.

**Reworking the cut without new shots.** Three more entry kinds go in the same `inserts.json` and apply in order with the others. `{"remove": "t28r"}` takes a shot out of the cut and moves everything after it back; its vocals go with it, and so do its board frames and previs unless `"keep_assets": true` parks them (the frames stay on the board under `"parked": "t28r"`, the previs under `previs.parked`). `{"move": "t30r", "insert_after": "t31r"}` puts a shot elsewhere with its own board frame (on its new number), previs and vocals; before this, a move was a remove and an insert by hand, and the frames were lost on the way. `{"restore": "t28r", "from": "plan.before.json", "insert_before": "t29"}` brings back a shot removed earlier, with its frames, previs and vocals as another plan file has them. Copy `plan.json.bak` aside first, because every run overwrites it. The rendered clip is still in `manifest.json` under the shot's label, so a moved or restored shot is not rendered again, and the summary says which shots were removed, moved or restored. Two more fix a take that is right except for a stretch: `{"trim": "t31r", "trim_seconds": 3.9}` gives a shot a new window in its take and moves the rest of the cut by the difference, and `{"split": "t33r", "cut_from": 0.35, "cut_to": 0.75}` drops that stretch (seconds of the take) and plays the rest as `t33r_2`, a hand-cut piece of the same clip. In the Tanya short, t33r flashed a stranger's clean room for half a second between the drawn first frame and the whip pan, and the split kept both good parts.

**A shot that opens on its board frame.** `"open_on_frame": true` sends the shot's own storyboard frame (`board/<frame id>.png`, the frame whose `"shot"` is the shot's index) to Seedance 2.5 as the first frame, image to video, with the body as the prompt and the bible's look after it. No portraits, place, voices or previs are sent; duration, resolution, aspect ratio and `generate_audio` stay as the shot has them. It replaces the hand-written `"bridge": {"from_image": "<output_dir>/board/fXX.png"}` of the Tanya short (t28r, t30r, t31r, t33r, t37r, t56b) and gives `reel_cast` the same request for each of them. `drama.validate` refuses such a shot without a board frame, `reel_cast` stops with "run reel_board first" if the frame is not drawn, the preflight leaves its previs alone, and `reel_previs` neither renders nor attaches a previs for it.

**A line's own loudness.** `"gain_db"` on a dialogue line (`shots[i].dialogue[j]`) or on a vocal (`plan.vocals[k]`) moves it in the cut's voice mix, between -12 and +12 dB (default 0). A dialogue line gets it after loudnorm, on top of the 5 dB lift for a line under 1.2 s, and a vocal gets it on top of its placement gain. `reel_listen` prints a suggestion under every flagged line, +4 when nothing was heard and +3 when the words came out wrong, with the exact path to set it on: `vocals[57].gain_db` or `shots[t03a].dialogue[0].gain_db`. An edited line is voiced again: the ElevenLabs take is cached by the text it sends with its voices, room and model, and a `performance` that no longer says the line is replaced by the new line after the performance's leading tags.

**Changing only the sound.** `reel_animatic --plan-file <plan> --clips --audio-only --yes` rebuilds the sound alone, the way the full run makes it: the lines on the same mouth marks (`shots/mouths.json`) and the same fit, the vocals, the soundtrack and its mix. It then lays the result on the picture already in `trailer.mp4`, copying the video stream without encoding it again. A line's `gain_db`, a vocal, a sound placement or a new take of a line need only this. Every full build writes the picture's signature to `trailer.picture.json`. That signature covers each part's inputs: the clip and its mtime, the window, speed, transitions, cards, grade, `hd`, `ending`, and the board frame of a shot not rendered yet. `--audio-only` compares the signature first and refuses, naming the shots, when the plan no longer makes that picture ("t30r, t31r changed; t34f new; t34 gone; a picture build is needed"). It never saves the plan. The full build keeps each encoded part in `parts/<key>.mp4` and joins the parts by stream copy, so after a trim only that shot and the shots it touches are encoded again. The score is kept across a retime: a music source is keyed without its section lengths, and each section of the take it has is stretched to its new length in the mix. To ask for a new take, change anything else in the source (`"take": 2`). The run asks for confirmation only when something will be bought: an exchange with no take, a vocal not yet voiced, or a sound source not yet made. Mixes whose inputs did not move are not run again, and file lengths are probed once per file version (`~/.cache/skills/probed.json`). In the Tanya short (95 parts, 5:19 at 1080p), drawing every part took about 5 minutes. A rerun with nothing changed took 19 seconds. `--audio-only` after one line's `gain_db` changed took about 100 seconds, most of it the soundtrack mix.

**Upscaling as clips land.** With `"hd": true` in the plan, `reel` sends every clip it has just rendered through Real-ESRGAN (`reel_upscale.upscale`, written to `shots/hd/`) before it goes on to the sound and the stitch, so the 1080p cut does not wait for a batch upscale. Clips carried over by `--resume` are left to `reel_upscale`. Without the Real-ESRGAN binary `reel` prints one line and carries on. The grade is applied in the cut, not here.

**Listen to the cut.** `python3 -m common.runners.cli.reel_listen --plan-file <series>/short/plan.json [--film <mp4>] [--only t03a,t08b] [--threshold 0.6]` cuts each line out of the finished film where it plays: a dialogue line at its shot's `clock_at` plus its `line_at`, and a vocal at its `at` if it contains a word ("Ура!", "Ещё!"; "А-а-ах!", "Тсс" and "Хып!" are skipped). It transcribes each line with a local Whisper (`mlx_whisper`, large-v3-turbo, Russian; free), compares the result with the line after normalising both (lowercase, ё read as е, no punctuation) and lists every line that scores under the threshold, with what was expected and what was heard. The film defaults to `<output_dir>/trailer.mp4`. Transcripts are cached in `listen-cache/` by the sound of each window (its samples, the Whisper model and the language), so a re-cut hears again only the lines whose sound changed, and the report is written to `listen.json`. In the Tanya short, short lines were misheard («Корт» heard as «Торт», «Ещё!» unspeakable, one-word baby lines under the music) and were caught only by ear. A full pass of the final cut (51 lines) takes about two minutes. Children's high voices transcribe poorly, and Whisper writes digits for numbers ("сорок" comes back as "40"), so a flag means listen to that line, not that it is wrong. The score is for the whole line, so a single wrong word in a long line (Таня heard as «Даня», 0.87) is not flagged: read `listen.json` for those.

**What each stage keeps between runs.** A rerun that changed nothing redoes nothing, and a change redoes only what it touches. `reel_cast` keeps every portrait, plate and voice in `cast.json` by the hash of its description, rewrites `plan.json` only when a scene's references or prompt actually changed, grabs a bridge's seam frames again only when the clip or the second they come from moved (`bridges/frames.json`), and prints in full only the preflight notes that are new since the last run (`preflight.json`). `reel_board` keeps each frame in `board/board.json` by its id, prompt, refs, spec and check, encodes a jpg again only after its png is redrawn, and makes the contact sheet again only when a frame on it changed (`board/cache.json`). `reel_previs` keeps each clay clip in `previs/cache.json` by its camera, lens, set, actors, length, frame size and the scene script: a rerun sends Blender only the shots whose spec moved (one changed lens took 4 seconds instead of 2 minutes 40 on the Tanya short), and the contact sheet and previs cut are made again only when a clip, a trim or the voice changed. `reel --resume` keeps every take in `manifest.json` by label and records what each was rendered from, so a take whose shot was edited since is named in the preview, never paid for again unasked. An `hd` copy is redone only when its clip is newer than the copy, so `reel`'s upscale on landing and `reel_upscale` never do the same clip twice. A vocal is kept by who says it, the text and the voice it is read in, so a speaker given a new voice is read again. `reel_listen` keeps transcripts by the sound itself.

## Directors

`common/video-prompt-library/directors/base.md` holds 24 rules for long narrative YouTube film. A series director extends it and states only what changes: `extends: base`, one sentence of style, then rules. A base number replaces that rule, 101 and up add new ones. A director can be reused across series by copying it into `directors/<id>.md` and extending it by id.

Why rules and not a style adjective: "shoot it like a thriller" gives the model nothing it can execute, while "average shot 2 s, hand-held on 24 mm, hard cut on every line" does. The DIRECTOR prompt rejects any rule a camera cannot carry out.
