# Examples — research-brief calibration

3 calibration runs showing input → expected output shape. Use to anchor your sense of brief depth and structure.

---

## Example 1 — Standard depth on a tech topic

### User request

> research-brief "AI productivity tools for solo founders in 2026" --for carousel

### Expected output (saved to file)

```markdown
# AI Productivity Tools For Solo Founders In 2026

_Brief generated: 2026-05-21. Sources used: 11. Depth: standard. Language: en._

## TL;DR

Solo founders in 2026 have shifted from agentic-everything experiments back to thin AI integrations layered on top of best-of-breed SaaS, with Notion-style hubs losing share to vertical tools. The biggest unlock is asynchronous voice + writing assistants that handle Slack / email triage without context-switching. The contrarian frame: most "AI productivity gains" reported in 2024 surveys were time-spent reorganizing prompts, not output.

## Key facts

- Per a16z's Q1 2026 founder survey, 73% of solo-founder respondents use 4+ AI tools daily; up from 41% in 2024. [#1]
- Voice-first tools (Wispr, Granola, etc.) are reported as highest-retention category among 1-person companies (NPS 60+). [#2][#3]
- Vertical AI tools (Cursor for code, Granola for meetings, Lex for writing) outperform horizontal hubs (Notion AI, ChatGPT general) on user-reported task completion. [#4]
- Stripe's solo-founder cohort grew 38% YoY in 2025, attributed in part to AI lowering ops overhead. [#5]
- The "agent stack" hype peaked Q3 2025; most published reviews now downgrade agentic tools below single-shot LLM use for solo work. [#6] [single-source]
- ChatGPT Pro and Claude Max are the two most-cited subscriptions in founder spend reports — combined median spend ~$60/mo. [#7]
- Common failure mode: founders adopt 8+ tools, end up spending 12+ hours/week on tool maintenance vs. work. [#8]
- 47% of surveyed founders report using AI for 1:1 customer support response drafting (Front, Pylon integrations). [#9]
- Anthropic and OpenAI both shipped "memory" features in 2025 that solo founders cite as eliminating ~3 hrs/week of context-rebuilding. [#1][#10]
- The "AI bookkeeper" category (Pilot, Bench AI tools) hasn't fully matured — multiple solo founders report still using a human accountant for tax. [#11] [single-source]

## Notable quotes

> "I used to live in Notion AI; now I have 6 vertical tools and never open the hub. Total cost is higher but I save 8 hours a week."
> — Anonymized solo founder quoted in a16z Q1 2026 founder survey [#1]

> "Agents work for things you don't care about. For things you care about, you still want to drive."
> — Posted by Linear cofounder Tuomas Artman on X, March 2026 [#6]

## Suggested angles

1. **The 4-tool stack that 73% of founders use** — for carousel
   - 8-slide breakdown of the typical AI stack with one tool per slide + retention reasons
   - Builds on [#1] [#4] [#7]

2. **Voice-first AI is winning** — for carousel
   - 7-slide narrative: why text-input AI plateaued, what voice tools enable, NPS data, founder quotes
   - Builds on [#2] [#3]

3. **Why agents lost the solo-founder market** — for carousel
   - 8-slide contrarian piece on the agent hype cycle peak + collapse, with Tuomas quote as hero
   - Builds on [#6] [#1]

## Open questions

- The 38% YoY growth in Stripe solo-founder cohort — how much is AI vs. macroeconomic post-2024 layoff founders entering market?
- No corroboration found for Granola's exact 2025 ARR (rumored multiple figures but unverified).
- "AI bookkeeper" tools — accuracy claims vary widely between vendor pages and independent reviews.

## Out of reach

- Solo-founder MRR distribution — paywalled (Stripe Atlas private data, Mercury internal reports).
- Founder LTV/CAC by AI tool spend — no published data; would need user's own customer data to model.

## Sources

1. [a16z Q1 2026 founder survey](https://a16z.com/...) — a16z Research, 2026-02-15 — accessed 2026-05-21
2. [Wispr Flow product report](https://wispr.com/...) — Wispr, 2026-03-10 — accessed 2026-05-21
3. [Granola NPS publication](https://granola.com/blog/...) — Granola, 2026-01 — accessed 2026-05-21
... (full list of 11 numbered sources)
```

### What to notice

- TL;DR is EXACTLY 3 sentences, with a contrarian frame.
- Every fact has a `[#N]` marker. One has `[single-source]`.
- Quotes have full attribution.
- 3 angles — each distinctly different shape (stack breakdown, trend narrative, contrarian).
- Open questions surface real gaps.
- Out-of-reach section is honest about paywall + unavailable data.

## Example 2 — Quick depth on a cultural topic

### User request

> research-brief "Stanley cup brand resurgence" --depth quick --for reel

### Expected output

```markdown
# Stanley Cup Brand Resurgence

_Brief generated: 2026-05-21. Sources used: 6. Depth: quick. Language: en._

## TL;DR

The Stanley brand — a century-old workwear vacuum-bottle company — became a TikTok-driven luxury accessory between 2023 and 2025, then plateaued in 2026 as the discourse turned from aspirational to satirical. The lift came from a single product (the 40oz Quencher H2.0) plus influencer placement at scale. The contrarian frame: most reporting credits "TikTok virality" but the actual lift was deliberate paid-marketing investment that the brand obscures.

## Key facts

- Stanley revenue rose from $73M (2019) to ~$750M (2023), led by the Quencher tumbler. [#1]
- Quencher H2.0 retail price stayed flat ($45) while secondary market peaked at $200+ for limited colors in 2024. [#2]
- The brand was acquired by PMI Worldwide in 1991 and reinvented under CEO Terence Reilly post-2020. [#1][#3]
- Female 30-50yo customer share went from ~15% (pre-2020) to ~70% (2023). [#4]
- Lead poisoning controversy in late 2024 — Stanley admitted lead used in vacuum-seal but contained in inaccessible space; sales dipped briefly, recovered. [#5]
- 2026 search interest is ~40% off the 2023 peak per Google Trends. [#6] [single-source]

## Suggested angles

1. **The marketing playbook nobody wants to admit** — for reel
   - 3-shot reel: surface story (TikTok virality) → reveal (paid placement strategy) → lesson (deliberate vs. organic)
   - Builds on [#1] [#3]

2. **A 40oz cup is a status symbol now** — for reel
   - 2-shot reel: the cup IS the post (visual hook) → narrative about identity-as-object
   - Builds on [#2] [#4]

3. **The Quencher plateau** — for reel
   - 3-shot: stat (40% decline in interest) → why (saturation + discourse fatigue) → what's next
   - Builds on [#6]

## Open questions

- Actual paid-marketing budget breakdown — Stanley doesn't disclose; one Adweek piece estimates but no confirmed figure.
- How much of the lead-controversy impact is permanent — sales recovered but brand sentiment data is gated.

## Sources

1. [The Real Story Behind Stanley's TikTok Rise](https://...) — Bloomberg, 2024-01-15 — accessed 2026-05-21
2. [Stanley Quencher resale data](https://...) — StockX blog, 2024-06-12 — accessed 2026-05-21
3. [How Stanley reinvented under Terence Reilly](https://...) — Fast Company, 2024-03-08 — accessed 2026-05-21
4. [Stanley demographics shift](https://...) — WSJ, 2024-02-21 — accessed 2026-05-21
5. [Lead in Stanley cups: what's actually in there](https://...) — Wirecutter, 2024-11-05 — accessed 2026-05-21
6. [Stanley search interest 2023-2026](https://trends.google.com/...) — Google Trends, accessed 2026-05-21
```

### What to notice

- Quick depth: 6 sources, no Quotes section (none found that meet criteria), no Out-of-reach (none needed).
- Open Questions still present — depth=quick doesn't skip them if there are real gaps.
- Angles tailored to reel format with shot counts.

## Example 3 — Deep on a niche business topic

### User request

> research-brief "vertical AI tools for veterinary clinics" --depth deep --format article-ready --for landing

### Expected output (truncated; full would be 1000-1500 words)

```markdown
# Vertical AI Tools For Veterinary Clinics

_Brief generated: 2026-05-21. Sources used: 24. Depth: deep. Language: en._

## TL;DR

Veterinary medicine has been one of the slowest-to-digitize healthcare adjacencies, and 2025-2026 marks an inflection: 4 well-funded startups now target the niche specifically (record-keeping, image triage, clinical decision support), with the largest (Otto, Closed Loop) raising A+ rounds in late 2025. The buying motion is solo-vet or small-clinic, sub-$200/mo per provider. The contrarian frame: most clinics report the bottleneck is still front-desk + insurance, not clinical AI — meaning the loudest startup category may be solving the wrong problem.

## Context (article-ready mode only)

[2-paragraph context block on why veterinary tech lagged + recent funding climate]

## Key facts

[15 bulleted facts with citations — same format as standard, but more granular]

## Notable quotes

[4 quotes from veterinary association reports + named clinicians]

## Suggested angles

[5 angles, each with a paragraph rationale — for landing-hero specifically]

## Open questions

[8 unresolved gaps]

## Out of reach

[Paywalled VPMA reports, vendor enterprise pricing, peer-reviewed Vet Med Today article]

## Sources

[24 numbered sources]
```

### What to notice

- Deep depth: 24 sources, includes primary (peer-reviewed journals + SEC filings of public corporate parents).
- Article-ready format: includes a Context section between TL;DR and Key facts.
- 8 open questions — deep mode surfaces more research gaps, not fewer.
- Out-of-reach is detailed — paywalled vet trade press, vendor enterprise pricing.
