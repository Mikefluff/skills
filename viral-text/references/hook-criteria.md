# Hook Criteria

Use this checklist whenever you construct or validate the headline (hook). Viral-specific banned constructions in hooks/CTAs are listed at the bottom — these go on top of `writer`'s universal bans.

## HOOK CRITERIA

Create a hook (headline) following these rules:
- Understandable to wide masses
- Controversial and provocative — evokes vivid emotions
- Sparks burning curiosity — makes you want to click and read
- Must contain a specific object or action (breakfast, gesture, phrase, habit)
- Polarizing — divides people into camps
- 7-12 words, no fluff
- Doesn't promise benefit — shows relief from a problem
- Realistic, verifiable
- Short bait bracket at the end (no commas inside)
- One word in CAPS for emphasis
- Ends with a colon
- Doesn't blame the reader
- Always unique, not templated
- Trust through specifics — numbers, facts
- Specific subject + place/context
- Different structural forms
- Unusual transfer verb
- Simple, everyday language
- Wide topics: body, food, sleep, habits, relationships, money
- Sophisticated phrasing but simple words
- Clear pain in focus
- Never "one man said"
- Secret/hidden knowledge effect
- Time marker for effect
- Rare/memorable character
- Action is easily repeatable
- Simple action → sharp effect

Hook structure: Character (archetype + location) + transfer verb (unusual) + secret/discovery + consequence/effect + time marker (optional) + bait bracket + colon

### Viral-specific banned constructions in hooks/CTAs (on top of writer's universal bans)

NEVER use in viral content:
- "Red flags / Green flags" lists without real insight
- Same rhythm across all paragraphs
- CTA cliches: "If this is about you, write YES", "write YES", "let's discuss it"
- Cliche metaphors: "works like a radar", "X calls X"
- Anything that "sounds like a typical viral post"

(All other prose-level bans — bureaucratic, calques, intensifiers, etc. — handled by writer.)
