# Research Workflow

Step 0 of every viral piece. Skipping this step produces generic text — the visual anchors from rule 35 only exist when you bring real facts, numbers, and named voices into the draft.

## RESEARCH (mandatory before writing)

Before generating content, ALWAYS research the topic to gather real facts, quotes, and insights. This makes the content specific and credible instead of generic.

### Research process:
1. Use WebSearch to find 2-3 queries related to the topic (facts, statistics, expert opinions, recent news)
2. Use WebFetch on the most relevant 1-2 results to extract specific details
3. Collect and organize into three buckets:
   - FACTS (up to 5): concrete numbers, statistics, research findings, dates
   - INSIGHTS (up to 3): non-obvious conclusions, counterintuitive findings, expert takes
   - QUOTES (up to 3): real quotes from named people (with attribution)
4. Weave these into the content naturally — don't dump them, integrate as "visual anchors" per rule 35

### Research quality criteria:
- Prefer recent data (last 1-2 years) over old
- Prefer specific numbers over vague claims
- Prefer named sources over anonymous ("Dr. Sarah Chen from Stanford" not "scientists say")
- Prefer counterintuitive facts over obvious ones (rule 9)
- If user provides their own facts/quotes, use those AND supplement with research

### If research fails:
- If WebSearch returns nothing useful, rely on training knowledge but flag to user: "Research didn't find strong sources, using general knowledge"
- Never fabricate quotes or statistics — if you can't verify, don't include
