# Edgar Wright · snap cuts

**Inspired by**: The body of work spanning *Hot Fuzz*, *Shaun of the Dead*, *Scott Pilgrim vs. the World*, *Baby Driver*, and *Last Night in Soho*. Use this style WITHOUT including the director's name in the actual model prompt — their name is for OUR taste, not the API.

**Cinematography anchor**: Rapid-fire visual comedy built on the rhythm of cuts, whip-pans, and snap-zooms timed to a sound effect on every transition. Each shot is brief — 0.3 to 2 seconds — and the camera moves with cartoonish exaggeration: a whip-pan races to the next setup with a swoosh, a snap-zoom punches into a face with a stinger, a match-cut links action across cuts so a thrown object becomes a thrown ball becomes a thrown punch. Lighting is bright, evenly lit, slightly saturated, with no shy shadows — comedy reads in clean light. Lenses are wide spherical primes that exaggerate depth and motion. Composition is centered for punch lines and aggressively low for hero shots. Production design pops with primary colors. The frame is always doing one thing very visibly so the cut works.

**Color palette**: saturated red, electric blue, warm yellow, clean white, punchy black.

**Lens & framing**: wide spherical primes 18mm/24mm/28mm exaggerating depth and motion, deep focus, 1.85:1 flat, centered for punch lines, low-and-wide for hero shots, occasional dutch tilt for chaos.

**Lighting**: bright and evenly lit, slightly saturated, no shy shadows, key/fill ratio close to 2:1. Practical sources visible in frame, fluorescents allowed, sun used as a wash not a spotlight.

**Motion language**: whip-pans triggered by sound cue. Snap-zooms in and out on a single axis. Crash-zooms to extreme close-up. Locked-off tableau cut against locked-off tableau. Occasional handheld run-along. Camera always doing something legible.

**Editing rhythm**: snap pacing, 30-60 cuts per minute. Cuts on a sound effect every time — door slam, footstep, drum hit. Match-cuts on shape, color, or action. Frequent micro-sequences (3-5 quick cuts) covering a single beat (waking up, making coffee, putting on a coat).

**Shot anchor (per-shot prompt fragment)**:
> 24mm wide spherical prime exaggerating depth and motion, deep focus, 1.85:1 flat aspect. Bright evenly-lit photography with key/fill ratio near 2:1, slightly saturated, no shy shadows, practical sources visible inside the frame. Saturated production-design palette of red, electric blue, warm yellow and clean white. Composition centered for punch lines or low-and-wide for hero entrances. Camera always doing one legible thing — a whip-pan racing to the next setup, a snap-zoom punching into a face, a crash-zoom to extreme close-up, or a locked-off tableau awaiting the next cut. Shot duration deliberately brief (under two seconds), every camera move and subject action staged to land on a sound cue. Atmosphere kinetic, comedic, percussive, action-driven.

**Action vocabulary**:
- Whip-pan from subject A to subject B in a single setup, triggered by a door slam
- Snap-zoom from medium to extreme close-up of subject's face on a reaction beat
- Crash-zoom into an object (kettle, phone, weapon) as it starts to do something
- Locked-off wide of subject sleeping, hard cut to locked-off wide of subject standing — same frame
- Match-cut on a thrown object linking two locations (a remote becomes a phone becomes a key)
- Five-cut micro-sequence: alarm, eyes opening, feet hitting floor, kettle clicking, door slamming
- Handheld run-along beside subject sprinting through a corridor
- Low-and-wide hero entrance: subject walks toward camera, lens at knee height, slight dutch tilt
- Insert: hands picking up an object in extreme close-up, snap-zoom timed to a thud
- Subject delivers a deadpan line to camera in single locked medium, cut on the punch word
- Whip-pan around a group of three subjects, each reacting in turn to the same off-screen sound
- Match-on-action: subject begins a movement (sip, punch, turn) and a different scene completes it

**Sound design implications**: dense layered foley — every action gets a sweetened sound (footsteps, kettle pop, fabric rustle, weapon click). Music is needle-drop driven, often a single track running across an entire sequence with cuts timed to its beats. Dialogue is sharp, fast, often interrupted by a sound cue.

**Best for**: comedy reel, branded explainer with energy, social-media product montage, kinetic CTA.

**Avoid for**: meditative narrative, intimate dialogue, austere luxury, slow-burn thriller.

**Suggested duration**: 4-shot × 3s for a snap montage, or 3-shot × 4s for a comedic micro-sequence.

**Suggested music style**: needle-drop-rock.
