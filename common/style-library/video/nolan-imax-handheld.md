# Nolan · IMAX handheld

**Inspired by**: The body of work spanning *Dunkirk*, *Interstellar*, *Tenet*, *Inception*, *The Dark Knight*, and *Oppenheimer*. Use this style WITHOUT including the director's name in the actual model prompt — their name is for OUR taste, not the API.

**Cinematography anchor**: Large-format 65mm photography rendering enormous depth and detail across the entire frame, with deep DoF that keeps both close foreground and distant horizon equally sharp. Lighting is naturalistic and motivated — overcast skies, real interior tungsten, real magic hour — never gelled or stylized. The grade is restrained: cool taupe and dust-blue mids, neutral skin, no orange-and-teal extremes. Camera is handheld but heavyweight, the operator's mass dampening the motion into a slow grounded sway rather than shake. Aspect ratio expands to a near-square 1.43:1 IMAX for vistas and grand reveals, contracts to 2.39:1 for intimate dialogue. Production design favors practical builds, real locations, real explosions, real horizons — anti-greenscreen physicality. Frames feel weighty, geographic, ledger-real.

**Color palette**: taupe, dust-blue, slate grey, neutral skin, faint ochre.

**Lens & framing**: 65mm large-format spherical primes (28mm, 35mm, 50mm equivalents), deep focus from foreground to horizon, aspect shifting between 2.39:1 anamorphic-flat and 1.43:1 IMAX near-square, eye-level for dialogue, low-and-wide for landscapes and architecture.

**Lighting**: natural motivated only — overcast daylight, real tungsten interior, real magic hour. Hard sun used as a wash, not a spotlight. Interior practicals (sconces, screens, monitors) are real working sources at real intensity. No gels, no extreme color grade.

**Motion language**: heavyweight handheld with operator mass dampening shake into a slow grounded sway. Cable-cam or helicopter wide aerials for geography reveals. Tracking dollies parallel to a moving vehicle or running subject. No drone-buzz, no whip pans, no zooms.

**Editing rhythm**: medium-to-urgent tempo, 10-16 cuts per minute, accelerating in action sequences via crosscutting between parallel timelines. Cuts on a ticking-clock beat. Long held wides for landscape reveal.

**Shot anchor (per-shot prompt fragment)**:

Subject grounded by gravity, breath visible in cold air, wardrobe textured by use. Atmosphere weighty, geographic, urgent.

**Action vocabulary**:
- Wide aerial reveal of a landscape (beach, desert, city skyline) holding for 6 seconds before subject enters
- Heavyweight handheld tracking shot following a running subject through debris or crowd
- Locked-off symmetrical wide of architectural interior, single subject crossing the frame
- Intimate handheld two-shot dialogue, foreheads close, both faces sharp in deep focus
- Slow push-in on subject's eye, reflection of an event visible in the iris
- Crosscut between two parallel actions in different locations, both at urgent tempo
- Wide low-angle of subject framed against open sky, wind catching clothing
- Vehicle-mounted track parallel to a moving car, both car and horizon in focus
- Interior of a control room, multiple working monitors as practical light sources
- Slow tilt up from boots in mud to a wide vista of distant action
- Subject walks toward an enormous practical effect (explosion, structure collapse) in the background
- Aspect-ratio expansion: shot opens at 2.39:1 widescreen and visibly opens to 1.43:1 on a reveal beat

**Sound design implications**: dialogue often buried in sound mix beneath wind, machinery, and score — deliberately. Wall-to-wall orchestral and synth score, brass-heavy, ticking-clock motifs. Foley is heavy and present (boots, breathing, machinery). Practical sound effects favored over sweeteners.

**Best for**: large-scale brand anthem, ambitious narrative trailer, vista-driven travel reel, urgent process documentary.

**Avoid for**: intimate confessional, comedy, low-budget single-room dialogue, anything requiring lightness or play.

**Suggested duration**: 4-shot × 6s for an anthem, or 3-shot × 8s for a slow-build trailer.

**Suggested music style**: cinematic-orchestral.
