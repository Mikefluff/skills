# Chazelle · musical glow

**Inspired by**: The body of work spanning *La La Land*, *Whiplash*, *Babylon*, and *First Man*. Use this style WITHOUT including the director's name in the actual model prompt — their name is for OUR taste, not the API.

**Cinematography anchor**: Heavily saturated primary-color photography evoking 1950s Technicolor musicals updated with contemporary digital long-take choreography. Lighting leans on saturated colored gels — pure cyan, pure magenta, pure amber — uplighting subjects against twilight skies during magic hour. The camera is a participant in the choreography, tracking dancers in unbroken takes, executing 360-degree pans around stationary figures, and craning up and over on jib or Steadicam to reveal scale. Lenses are spherical primes, medium DoF, lens flares allowed and welcomed. Composition is centered for solo performers, paired-mirror for duets, and wide-tableau for ensemble. The frame breathes with the music — push-ins land on downbeats, pans complete on melodic resolutions, cuts happen on percussive hits. Practical magic-hour skies dominate the backdrop.

**Color palette**: cyan, magenta, amber, deep navy twilight, white-hot key.

**Lens & framing**: 32mm and 40mm spherical primes, medium DoF, 2.39:1 widescreen, centered for solo performance, paired-mirror for duets, wide-tableau for ensembles, low angles favoring sky and stage.

**Lighting**: saturated gels — pure cyan, pure magenta, pure amber — used as uplight or backlight against magic-hour twilight skies. Hard key from a single source modeling the subject. Practical lens flares from a light bulb or sun edge allowed in frame. White-hot followspot for solo moments.

**Motion language**: Steadicam and crane choreography. Long unbroken takes 20-60 seconds. 360-degree pans orbiting stationary subjects. Jib rises from ground-level to overhead. Camera tracks alongside dancing subjects in mirror or parallel motion. Push-ins time-locked to musical downbeats.

**Editing rhythm**: kinetic but built on long takes — 3-6 long takes per minute, each holding 8-20 seconds, with cuts landing on percussive hits or musical resolutions. Match-cuts on motion (a hand thrown up, a spin completed, a curtain swept aside).

**Action vocabulary**:
- Steadicam tracks alongside a walking subject for a continuous 20-second take, magic-hour backdrop
- 360-degree orbital pan around a stationary subject standing in pure cyan uplight
- Jib rise from ground-level boots to wide overhead reveal of an ensemble tableau
- Subject spins, camera spins in counter-direction, both completing on a downbeat
- Locked low-angle wide of subject silhouetted against twilight sky, single magenta backlight
- Steadicam follows a duet through three rooms in a single take, palette shifting per room
- Push-in to subject's face on a held vocal note, lens flare crossing the frame
- Match-cut on a thrown hat or scarf between two parallel locations
- Wide tableau of ensemble freeze-framed, then subject begins moving alone in foreground
- Crane descent from sky to ground-level meeting of two subjects, both lit by amber practical
- Practical-only night scene under string lights, subject dances through hanging bulbs
- Reflection shot: subject performs facing a mirror, camera tracks both subject and reflection

**Sound design implications**: music-led — composition is built around a recurring piano or trumpet motif, full live-band orchestration on peak moments. Foley emphasizes physical performance (footfalls, breath, tap-shoes, snare). Dialogue is sparse and often half-sung.

**Best for**: branded musical reel, romantic narrative micro-film, performance capture, anthem-driven product reveal with motion.

**Avoid for**: surveillance/thriller, deadpan comedy, technical explainer, dialogue-heavy interview.

**Suggested duration**: 1-shot × 12s for a single long-take number, or 3-shot × 8s for a romantic montage.

**Suggested music style**: cinematic-jazz.
