# Iñárritu · long-take handheld

**Inspired by**: The body of work spanning *Birdman*, *The Revenant*, *Babel*, *Biutiful*, and *21 Grams*. Use this style WITHOUT including the director's name in the actual model prompt — their name is for OUR taste, not the API.

**Cinematography anchor**: Long unbroken take photography executed on Steadicam and shoulder-rig, the camera following the subject through real space at near-walking distance for tens of seconds to minutes. Lenses are wide spherical primes — 14mm to 21mm — that exaggerate depth and let the camera move close to a face without losing geography. Lighting is natural, supplemented only by practicals that exist inside the location. The grade is restrained, with cold blue or warm tungsten cast depending on environment. Camera follows the subject's body — over the shoulder, alongside the face, then orbiting around for a reverse — flowing without cut. Breath, sweat, and physical effort are foregrounded. Composition is reactive and close, allowing the subject to fill the frame and the world to peel by behind. Atmosphere is immersive, raw, with the audience tethered to the protagonist's physical experience.

**Color palette**: cold blue exterior, warm tungsten interior, neutral natural skin, mud-brown earth tones, blood-red accent.

**Lens & framing**: very wide spherical primes 14mm/18mm/21mm, deep focus, 1.85:1 flat or 2.39:1 widescreen, close to the subject's face filling the frame, geography unfolding in the background, low-and-close hero angle.

**Lighting**: natural light supplemented only by practicals that exist in the location — fire, lamps, screen glow, sun streaming through windows. Grade is restrained, leaning cold blue for exteriors and warm tungsten for interiors. No fill, no studio sources.

**Motion language**: Steadicam and shoulder-rig long takes. Camera follows subject's body at near-walking distance, transitioning from over-the-shoulder to alongside-the-face to reverse-orbit without cutting. Run-along through corridors and exteriors. Occasional locked-off moment when subject pauses, then resumes following.

**Editing rhythm**: long-take dominant — 1-3 cuts per minute, individual shots routinely 30-120 seconds. Hidden cuts (whip-pan through dark, push through doorway) used to extend the appearance of continuity. Hard cuts only on emotional ruptures.

**Action vocabulary**:
- Steadicam follows subject running through a corridor for 30 seconds, no cut, geography unfolding
- Reverse-orbit around subject's face mid-conversation, two-shot becoming one-shot becoming reverse
- Long take of subject preparing for action (tying boots, loading, breathing), camera circling slowly
- Subject crawls or struggles through environment, camera close to face, breath fogging the lens
- Through-doorway hidden cut: camera follows subject into darkness, emerges in a new location
- Handheld track alongside subject riding a horse, vehicle or running, both in deep focus
- Subject collapses to the ground, camera descends with them to floor level, no cut
- Whip-pan from face to environment to face again, all within a single take
- Subject delivers a monologue to camera while walking backward through a real space
- Long-take fight or struggle covered in one unbroken shot, camera staying close to bodies
- Slow orbit around a stationary subject in firelight, full 360 degrees over 25 seconds
- Insert close-up of hands trembling in extreme close, breath audible, single light source

**Sound design implications**: production sound dominant — breath, footfall, fabric, weapon clicks, environmental ambience all foregrounded. Score is sparse and percussive, often single-instrument (drum, cello, indigenous percussion). Dialogue is naturalistic, sometimes in multiple overlapping languages.

**Best for**: immersive narrative trailer, athletic performance reel, raw documentary recreation, founder-journey micro-film.

**Avoid for**: deadpan comedy, choreographed musical, austere static luxury, controlled-studio product shot.

**Suggested duration**: 1-shot × 15s for a single long-take immersion, or 2-shot × 10s for paired physical sequences.

**Suggested music style**: percussive-sparse.
