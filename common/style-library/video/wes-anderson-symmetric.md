# Wes Anderson · symmetric pastel

**Inspired by**: The body of work spanning *The Grand Budapest Hotel*, *The French Dispatch*, *Asteroid City*, *Moonrise Kingdom*, and *The Royal Tenenbaums*. Use this style WITHOUT including the director's name in the actual model prompt — their name is for OUR taste, not the API.

**Cinematography anchor**: Planimetric staging — every frame is built on a single axis perpendicular to the camera. Subjects are dead-centered or split-symmetrical, backgrounds are flat-on like proscenium theatre walls. Lighting is soft, even, almost shadowless, recalling children's-book illustration. Color is custom-mixed pastel — washed pinks, mustard yellows, mint, dusty teal — laid into production design rather than added in grade. Camera motion is restricted to four primitives: 90-degree whip pans between symmetrical setups, slow horizontal trucks parallel to a wall, snap zooms on a fixed axis, and overhead god-shots looking straight down at hands or tabletops. Faces are framed as if mounted: chin centered, equal headroom, expression deadpan. Aspect is often 1.37:1 Academy or 1.85:1 flat, never anamorphic. Everything reads as a hand-built diorama photographed in available light.

**Color palette**: dusty pink, mustard yellow, mint green, navy, cream.

**Lens & framing**: 27mm, 32mm and 40mm spherical primes, deep focus (f/8-f/11 look), centered composition with the subject's face on the optical axis, 1.85:1 flat or 1.37:1 Academy ratio, ground-line low or straight-on eye-level.

**Lighting**: soft, even, almost shadowless toplight imitating overcast daylight, key/fill ratio close to 1:1, no dramatic modeling, color comes from production design not gels, occasional motivated practical (lamp, signage, sconce) sitting symmetrically inside frame.

**Motion language**: locked tripod by default. Whip pans 90 or 180 degrees between two symmetrical setups. Slow horizontal trucks parallel to a flat back wall. Snap zooms in/out on a fixed axis. Overhead 90-degree-down inserts of hands, objects, papers. No handheld, no dutch tilts.

**Editing rhythm**: medium tempo, roughly 8-14 cuts per minute. Cuts are clean, often on the whip-pan or on a character finishing a line of dialogue. Chapter cards, intertitles, and dossier inserts break scenes. Occasional one-shot tableaux held for 6-10 seconds.

**Shot anchor (per-shot prompt fragment)**:
> Centered planimetric composition, subject placed dead on the optical axis with equal headroom and equal negative space left and right. 35mm spherical lens look, deep focus, 1.85:1 flat aspect. Soft even toplight, key/fill ratio 1:1, no dramatic shadows, a single warm motivated practical visible inside the frame. Pastel production-design palette of dusty pink, mustard yellow, mint, navy and cream baked into wardrobe, walls and props. Background reads as a flat proscenium of hand-painted theatre. Camera locked on a sturdy tripod or executing one of three primitive moves only — a 90-degree whip pan, a slow horizontal truck parallel to the back wall, or a snap zoom on the fixed axis. Subjects perform deadpan, eyes to lens or in profile. Quiet diorama atmosphere, hand-built and meticulously dressed.

**Action vocabulary**:
- Subject walks into frame from screen-left, stops dead-center, looks straight to lens, mouths a line
- Slow horizontal truck along a long flat wall revealing labeled doors or compartments in series
- 90-degree whip pan between two symmetrical setups of two characters facing each other
- Overhead 90-degree-down insert of hands arranging objects on a tabletop
- Snap zoom from wide tableau to medium close-up of single object on a desk
- Two characters seated dead-center of frame, identical posture, mirror-symmetrical wardrobe
- Slow dolly-in along the optical axis toward a doorway opening to a second symmetrical room
- Group portrait, six to eight subjects evenly spaced, all facing camera, equal headroom
- Whip pan triggered by a sound cue (slammed door, ringing bell), revealing identical adjacent room
- Static medium shot held for 8 seconds, subject's only motion is a slow blink or single nod
- Color-coded prop swap inside locked frame — hand enters, exchanges object, exits
- Pull-out reveal: tight insert on a label or letter, dolly back to discover the entire dollhouse interior

**Sound design implications**: dialogue is foregrounded, dry and close-miked with minimal room tone. Scored almost wall-to-wall — chamber strings, baroque harpsichord, vintage 60s pop needle-drops. Foley is loud and precise (typewriter keys, a tea-cup placed, a stamp pressed). Silence sits where music doesn't.

**Best for**: deadpan brand monologue, list-driven explainer reel, hand-built product reveal, twee narrative micro-doc.

**Avoid for**: sports edits, action sequences, gritty interviews, anything requiring naturalism or improvisation.

**Suggested duration**: 4-shot × 5s for chaptered reveal, or 1-shot × 8s for a single deadpan tableau.

**Suggested music style**: baroque-chamber.
