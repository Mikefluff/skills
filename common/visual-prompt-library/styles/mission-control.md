# MISSION CONTROL (CONTROL-LOOP DOCTRINE)

## Reference orientation

The aesthetic IS the math of management — Apollo-era mission-control pit, dispatcher's console of toggle switches and CRT monitors, every executor is a separate blinking red channel on the back wall demanding decisions, intercom questions, mid-task re-briefs. Vs one clean steady green channel labeled OWNER · DELIVERED that needs no intervention. The rhetorical wedge is the **count of lit channels** — the executor-heavy console is overloaded with red alerts; the owner-heavy console has one steady green and everything else dark. The style does not insult executors — it merely shows the load they impose. Cold dispatcher's gravity, not contempt.

## When NOT to use

- Cinematic sci-fi / spaceship-bridge content — this style is workplace-doctrine, not cinema.
- Cyberpunk dashboard / startup-vaporware — use `vibecoded-exposed` instead.
- General critique / debunk — use `brutalist` or `grunge`.
- Executor-as-villain framing — the style respects executors as a category; it just shows the operating-loop cost.

## Variation hints

- **Channel-count rhetoric is the heart**: every slide should anchor the comparison to LIT vs DARK channels on the back panel — 10 red alerts (executor load) vs 1 green (owner channel). Without the channel-count visual, the style collapses.
- **Red yarn schematic is the contrast asset**: corkboard with red yarn drawing CIRCULAR control loops between BOSS and each EXECUTOR (each loop = each task that has to return for decisions) vs a single STRAIGHT line from BOSS → OWNER → DELIVERED. Make the loops chaotic; make the straight line empty space.
- **CRT amber pool for the dispatcher's face**: gives the operator the patient-gravity look. Warm tungsten lamp adds the institutional cosiness.
- **The single green channel beats the maximalism**: a slide that's mostly dark consoles with ONE small steady green LED labeled "OWNER · DELIVERED" reads stronger than a slide that crowds the green channel with explanation.
- **Character role**: character is the SENIOR DISPATCHER / FLIGHT CONTROLLER / OPERATIONS CHIEF seated at the console, brass desk-mike at hand, clipboard with TASK STATUS rows on his lap. Quiet weight of someone who has worked through decades of executor-load math. NEVER smiling — only the patient gravity of someone watching the channel count. Cold-eyed, CRT-amber-lit, three-quarter pose.

## Real-world references for orientation only

Think the aesthetic of Apollo 11 Mission Control circa 1969 — matte-black consoles, amber-green CRT monitors, brass-bezel toggle switches, headsets with woven cords, cigarette smoke-haze under task-lamps, paper-tape teletype output. Or a power-plant dispatcher's station at the height of a grid-load event. The style does NOT mimic NASA / specific agencies — it borrows the *posture* of patient dispatcher gravity watching a load board.

Do NOT inject NASA badges, named astronauts, named missions, named corporations, or any specific real-world institutional logos into image prompts. Keep references in this file only.
