# CLINICAL DEBUNK / MISDIAGNOSIS

## Reference orientation

Treats pseudo-therapy / coaching language as **misdiagnosis** by a fake practitioner. The aesthetic is a real medical chart / clinical case file / prescription pad — cold, official, unforgiving — used to expose blame-shifting tactics like "fear of being seen", "haven't allowed yourself big money", "you're sabotaging scale". The fake diagnosis gets stamped red MISDIAGNOSIS; the real diagnosis (actual business mechanics like unit economics, traffic, sales process) is listed beneath as the correct chart entry.

## When NOT to use

- Genuine therapeutic content — would read as cruel parody of real therapy. Use `nature` or `nordic-minimal` instead.
- Wellness / mindfulness brands — same problem.
- Hope-driven narratives — too cold and unforgiving.
- Children's content / soft pedagogy — wrong register entirely.

## Variation hints

- **Typography stays clinical-cold**: NEVER mix in warm-empathetic typography like script or copperplate — that breaks the contrast. Clinical sans + serif case-report + monospace vital-signs ONLY.
- **Single-accent red discipline**: red is for stamps + corrections only. The body content stays black-on-cream / navy-on-cream. Two competing reds dilute the prosecutorial energy.
- **Use the vital-signs panel**: a monospace readout panel ("ВЫРУЧКА: ↓ 47% / CAC: ↑ 3.2× / RETENTION: 12%") as the "actual diagnosis" line items is the rhetorical heart of this style.
- **Stamps go DIAGONAL**: MISDIAGNOSIS / REJECTED / EXAMINED stamps are always angled 10–20° — never horizontal. The diagonal is what makes them read as official rubber stamps, not labels.
- **Character role** (if a character is included): the character is the DIAGNOSTICIAN — holding a clipboard, examining a folder, signing a prescription, tapping a chart with a pen. NEVER the "patient" being misdiagnosed. The character is the second opinion who corrects the fake one.

## Real-world references for orientation only

Think the cold institutional aesthetic of a real medical case report or insurance-claim rejection letter — *Lancet* journal page, FDA notice, GAO audit report. The style does NOT mimic any specific publication's house style; it borrows the *posture* of clinical authority overruling pseudo-therapy.

Do NOT inject named publications, named clinicians, or named accused individuals into image prompts — keep references in this file only.
