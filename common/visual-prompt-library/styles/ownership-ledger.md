# OWNERSHIP LEDGER (TAKEN-BY DOCTRINE)

## Reference orientation

The aesthetic IS the doctrine — cold executive ledger paper, fountain-pen signature lines, wax seals, contract stamps, brass-lamp pool of light on a mahogany desk. The rhetorical wedge is the SIGNATURE LINE: either it's signed in fountain-pen ink (TAKEN BY) or it's left blank and stamped red (ADVISED ONLY · NO COUNTERSIGNATURE / INVALID). The style does not theatricize — it weighs. Think Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter aesthetic crossed with a Christopher Nolan corporate-drama lighting setup. The viewer should feel they're being shown a real partner-grade document, not an Instagram graphic.

## When NOT to use

- Hype / startup / TikTok-energy content — the style is deliberately slow and serious; would read as boring.
- Wellness / self-help / lifestyle — wrong register; this is for accountability-doctrine, not vibes.
- Apocalyptic / aggressive critique — use `brutalist` or `grunge` instead; this style's edge is paper-weight, not concrete-fist.

## Variation hints

- **Signature line is the rhetorical heart**: every assertion the carousel makes must visually anchor to either a signed line (validated) or a blank line stamped red (invalid). Without that anchor the style collapses to "old document".
- **TAKEN BY column ALWAYS load-bearing**: when comparing owner vs advisor, the TAKEN BY column on the owner's side is signed in bold fountain-pen ink with a date, and on the advisor's side it's left blank with a red diagonal stamp. The contrast is the doctrine.
- **Wax seals = closure**: any slide that delivers a verdict gets a red wax-seal "OWNED" or "DELIVERED" embossed in the corner. Verdicts without seals look like proposals; with seals they look like rulings.
- **Character role**: character is the SENIOR MANAGING PARTNER / CHAIRMAN / FINANCIAL TRUSTEE reviewing the ledger from behind the desk. Holds either a classical fountain pen, a sealed envelope, or the contract folder. NEVER smiling — only the quiet gravity of someone signing off on consequence. Cold-eyed, brass-lamp-lit, three-quarter pose.

## Real-world references for orientation only

Think the aesthetic of a private-bank trustee's office circa 1962 — heavy cream paper, fountain-pen signature lines, red wax seals, brass desk lamp, walnut paneling. Or a Berkshire-Hathaway partner letter laid out for signature on a partner's desk. The style does NOT mimic any specific bank, trust, or law firm — it borrows the *posture* of paper-and-ink accountability against the modern noise of advice without signature.

Do NOT inject named banks, named law firms, named family offices, or named luxury-good brands into image prompts. Keep references in this file only.
