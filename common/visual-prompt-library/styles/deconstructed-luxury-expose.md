# DECONSTRUCTED LUXURY / EXPOSÉ

## Reference orientation

Think prosecutor's evidence board mounted on top of a luxury magazine spread. The dominant emotional note is *the trap and the unmasking on the same canvas* — the slide LOOKS like infogypsy promo material (champagne, gold foil, Dubai postcards, sports cars) but is entirely deface'd with hand-drawn red marker (circles, X-strikes, EXHIBIT stamps, verdict words, FAKE stamps). The visual IS the message: "here's the lie they sell, and here's why it's a lie".

## When NOT to use

- Sincere luxury / actual premium brand work — would read as parody and damage the brand. Use `art-deco` or `glamour` instead.
- Wellness / mindful content — too aggressive and prosecutorial. Use `nature` or `nordic-minimal`.
- Children's content / hopeful narratives — the cynicism is wrong register.
- Anything where the user actually IS the expert being celebrated — the style is for unmasking *other* people's bullshit, not for self-celebration.

## Variation hints

- **Single-accent discipline**: the screaming-red marker is the ONE accent color. Don't add a second highlight color — it dilutes the prosecutorial energy.
- **Gold foil under-layer ALWAYS**: the gold / champagne / luxe elements are ALWAYS the bottom layer (the lie). Red marker is ALWAYS on top (the unmasking). Never invert the layering — that breaks the conceptual premise.
- **Character role**: if a character is included, they are the PROSECUTOR / detective / amused observer pointing at the evidence — NEVER one of the exhibits being mocked. Place them off-center looking at the viewer with a knowing skeptical expression.
- **Use exhibit-counting**: when listing multiple props/sins/red-flags, label them EXHIBIT A / B / C / D — the courtroom framing reinforces the prosecutorial mood.
- **Receipt closure**: for the final / CTA slide, use the itemized-receipt-strip composition with a handwritten red-marker total at the bottom. It's the most cutting closer the style offers.
- **Verdict words discipline**: one-word red marker verdicts ("декорация", "витрина", "эмоция", "ложь", "пустота") under each exhibit are the rhetorical heart of the style — use them as recurring annotations, not just decoration.

## Real-world references for orientation only

This is the aesthetic of investigative journalism overlaying luxury magazine spreads — think exposé features in *Vanity Fair* or *The New Yorker* where photo collages of suspect lifestyles are annotated by an off-page critic. The style does NOT mimic any specific publication's house style; it borrows the *posture* of investigative annotation atop glossy source material.
