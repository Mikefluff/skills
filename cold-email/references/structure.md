# Cold-email structure

The 5-block template, adapted per email type, plus subject-line rules.

---

## The 5 blocks (default — first-touch)

1. **Hook** (1 sentence). Why this email exists, written from the recipient's perspective. The recipient should know within 12 words why they should keep reading.
2. **Value / context** (1-2 sentences). Concrete proof that you're not wasting their time. Numbers, specifics, prior result, a piece of their work you actually engaged with.
3. **The ask** (1 sentence). One specific thing. Time-bounded if possible.
4. **Easy yes** (1 sentence, optional). Lower friction — "even 10 min on Zoom", "or just a yes/no — happy to expand by email".
5. **Sign-off** (1 line). Name + minimal context (role + 1-2 keywords). Optional: link to your portfolio / company.

**Total**: 4-6 sentences. ~80-120 words.

### Example skeleton (first-touch to a VC)

> Hi {first name},
>
> {1 — Hook: why now, specific to their portfolio / fund thesis}
>
> {2 — Value: concrete proof: metric, prior round, customer name, market wedge}
>
> {3 — Ask: 20 min next week to walk through the deck?}
>
> {4 — Easy yes: happy to send the deck cold if Zoom is too much.}
>
> Best,
> {Name}
> {Title, Company} · {one-line tagline} · {link}

## Variant — `follow-up`

Recipient didn't reply to the first email. Do NOT repeat the first email. Add ONE thing:

1. **Pointer to original** (1 sentence). "Following up on the {date} note about {topic}."
2. **Why now** (1 sentence). New context that justifies the bump: a recent shipping, a closed round, a relevant news item, a deadline.
3. **Re-ask, simplified** (1 sentence). Either the same ask or a smaller one.
4. **Sign-off.**

**Total**: 3-4 sentences. ~50-80 words.

Do NOT:
- apologize for following up ("sorry to bother you again")
- restate the original pitch
- use "just bumping this up"
- send more than 2 follow-ups before stopping

---

## Variant — `intro-request`

You are asking a mutual contact (the intro-giver) to introduce you to a third party (the target). Two emails:

**Email A — to the intro-giver:**

1. **Direct ask** (1 sentence). "Would you be open to introducing me to {target}?"
2. **Why them, specific** (1 sentence). Why the target in particular.
3. **The forwardable** (below the line). A separately-formatted block the intro-giver can paste into a new thread with the target.

**Email B — the forwardable** (the block in email A):

> Subject: {forwardable subject — see section 6}
>
> Hi {target first name},
>
> {intro-giver's name} suggested we should connect.
>
> {1 — Hook: who you are, ≤15 words}
>
> {2 — Value: 1 sentence of proof}
>
> {3 — Ask: one specific thing}
>
> Happy to send more if useful. Otherwise no obligation — thanks {intro-giver's first name}.
>
> {Name}
> {Title, Company} · {link}

The forwardable is what gets read by the target. Optimize it harder than email A.

---

## Variant — `re-engage`

You had a conversation N months/years ago and want to re-open.

1. **Reference the prior thread** (1 sentence). Brief, no nostalgia. "We talked at {context} about {topic} in {month}."
2. **What changed** (1-2 sentences). Why now, specifically. Your milestone or theirs.
3. **The ask** (1 sentence). Often softer than first-touch — "would love to catch up", "20 min if you have it", "would value your read on X".
4. **Sign-off.**

**Total**: 3-5 sentences. ~60-100 words.

---

## Variant — `forwardable` (standalone)

When you are asked to write the forwardable email that another person will paste. Use the structure from `intro-request` Email B. Optimize ruthlessly — the intro-giver may add a sentence or two, so leave room.

**Total**: ≤80 words.

---

## 6. Subject lines

The subject line is the first hook. It must do one of:
- name a shared context ("{Mutual name} mentioned you")
- promise a specific outcome ("3-line ask: {topic}")
- be a clear question ("Open to {action}?")
- name the value upfront ("{Metric} for {their company}")

**Banned subject lines**:
- "Quick question" — empty calorie; recipient assumes spam
- "Touching base" — corporate cringe
- "Hi" / "Hello {name}" — looks like marketing automation
- "Following up" without specifics — same as above
- "Opportunity to {anything}" — sales-spam vibe
- "Are you the right person at {company}?" — recruiter / SDR signature
- Anything ALL CAPS or with `[URGENT]` / `[!]`
- Emoji in subject — exceptions only when matching your audience's culture

**Length cap**: 50 characters (so it doesn't truncate on mobile previews).

---

## Sign-off

Default:
> Best,
> {First Last}
> {Title, Company} · {link}

**Alternatives** (use when they match your voice and the recipient's culture):
- "Thanks, {Name}" — when ending with an ask
- "Best, {Name}" — neutral default
- "{Name}" — when you've already established multi-message rapport

**Banned**:
- "Warm regards" / "Kind regards" / "Sincerely" — too formal for cold outreach
- "Cheers" — only if it matches your normal voice
- "Yours faithfully" — never in cold outreach
- "TYIA" / "TIA" — looks needy

**Title line**: keep to one line. "CEO, Acme · acme.co" is fine. Don't list 4 things.

---

## Notes on first-line opening

After the subject, the first line of the body sets the tone. Two acceptable openings:

1. **Direct**: "Hi {Name}, {hook}".
2. **Anchored to context**: "Hi {Name}, I saw {specific thing they shipped / wrote / announced}. {hook}."

Do NOT:
- "Hope you're doing well." — strip on sight
- "I hope this email finds you well." — strip on sight
- "I came across your profile and ..." — generic, never specific enough
- "I'll keep this brief." — proves you can't write brief
- "I know you're busy, so I'll get right to the point." — already failed to get to the point
- "I'm reaching out because ..." — passive, distant

Be direct. The recipient will respect it.
