# Follow-up — calibration

After a non-response. Do NOT repeat the original. Add ONE new thing that justifies the bump.

---

## Scenario — founder → VC (follow-up to Scenario 1 in outreach.md)

**Context.** First email sent 9 days ago. No response. The fund just announced a $200M fund close — news the founder can anchor the bump to.

### Before (generic, ~110 words)

> Subject: Re: Quick question
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Just wanted to bump this up to the top of your inbox. I know you must be incredibly busy, so apologies for the follow-up.
>
> I sent you an email last week about Acme — we're a B2B SaaS company in the manufacturing-operations space that's been growing rapidly. I'd really love to set up a quick call to discuss potential opportunities.
>
> If now isn't a good time, I completely understand — would love to circle back in a few weeks.
>
> Looking forward to your response!
>
> Best,
> John Smith

### After (~70 words)

> Subject: Re: $1.2M ARR in manufacturing ops — fit for Acorn?
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> Following up on the {date} note. Saw the Acorn-IV close — congrats. With the new fund I'd guess your A pipeline is opening up.
>
> We hit $1.4M ARR this week (+$200K since the first email). Still planning a Q3 round; 20 min next week worth it?
>
> Or a "no" is fine — won't follow up further.
>
> Best,
> John

### Diff in deltas

- Removed "Just wanted to bump this up" (-7)
- Removed "apologies for the follow-up" (-5)
- Removed "incredibly busy" pity-framing (-5)
- Re-stated original ASK in fewer words; did not re-explain Acme
- Anchored to NEW news (Acorn-IV fund close)
- Added one new datapoint (+$200K ARR since first email)
- Offered the no-out ("a 'no' is fine — won't follow up further")
- Subject kept original threading (`Re:` + original subject)

## Scenario — candidate → recruiter (follow-up)

**Context.** First email sent 6 days ago. No response. The candidate just got merged into open-source project relevant to the company's stack.

### Before (~100 words)

> Subject: Re: Application for Staff Engineer role
>
> Hi Maria,
>
> I hope you're doing well!
>
> I just wanted to follow up on my application from last week for the Staff Engineer role. I'm still very interested in the position and would love to hear back.
>
> Please let me know if there's any additional information I can provide. I'm happy to answer any questions you may have.
>
> Looking forward to your reply!
>
> Best,
> Alex Park

### After (~55 words)

> Subject: Re: Staff Engineer — payments infra background
>
> Hi Maria,
>
> One update on the Staff Engineer application: my PR for the {OSS-project-name} retries-budget feature merged Friday — relevant to the work in Finchat's payments infra.
>
> Still keen. Worth a conversation?
>
> Best,
> Alex

### Diff in deltas

- Removed "I hope you're doing well" (-5)
- Removed "I just wanted to follow up" / "still very interested" (-12)
- Added ONE concrete new thing (OSS merge)
- Directly relevant to their role (not just any update)

## Scenario — writer → editor (follow-up)

**Context.** First pitch sent 12 days ago, no response. A competing publication just covered a tangential story — the writer's piece would have a sharper angle.

### Before (~120 words)

> Subject: Re: Article pitch
>
> Dear Mr. Thompson,
>
> I hope this email finds you well.
>
> I'm following up on the pitch I sent on {date} for the longform on "ghost-shifts" in warehouse logistics. I wasn't sure if you had a chance to review it yet, so I wanted to check in.
>
> I noticed The Verge ran a piece yesterday on related unpaid-overtime patterns, which I think confirms there's appetite for this story. My piece would go deeper — internal documents, twelve worker interviews, specific corporate policy.
>
> Please let me know if this is something you'd be open to discussing further.
>
> Sincerely,
> Lena Volkov

### After (~75 words)

> Subject: Re: Pitch: "Ghost-shifts" — unpaid overtime as warehouse policy
>
> Hi Mr. Thompson,
>
> Following up on the {date} pitch. The Verge ran a surface-level piece on adjacent unpaid-overtime yesterday — confirms appetite, but they don't have the documents or workers.
>
> If The Foundry wants the deeper version (12 interviews, internal HR memo, two companies named), I can have a first draft in 3 weeks.
>
> Otherwise no further nudges from me.
>
> Best,
> Lena Volkov

### Diff in deltas

- Removed "Dear Mr. Thompson" / "I hope this email finds you well" (-9)
- Removed "I wasn't sure if you had a chance to review it yet" (-11)
- Compressed the The-Verge anchor + differentiation in one sentence
- Added concrete delivery commitment ("first draft in 3 weeks")
- Gave the out ("Otherwise no further nudges from me")

---

## Rules from these examples

1. **One new thing per follow-up.** News, milestone, datapoint, external event.
2. **Do not re-explain the original pitch.** Trust that the recipient can scroll up if they care.
3. **Subject line: keep original `Re:` threading.** Don't change the subject — that fragments the thread for them.
4. **End with the out.** "Or a 'no' is fine — won't follow up further" / "Otherwise no further nudges from me". This is the highest-ROI line in the email.
5. **Max 2 follow-ups, then stop.** A third follow-up is harassment.
