# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is acting in good faith until they prove otherwise. Criticise the work, not the person.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, and any other space where this project is being worked on — including private correspondence when it concerns the project.

## What is expected

- Technical disagreement is welcome and useful. Personal attacks, condescension and sneering are not.
- Assume the person filing a confused issue is confused, not stupid. Most confusion is a documentation bug.
- Feedback on a contribution addresses the contribution: what it does, what it breaks, what would make it mergeable.
- Respect that maintainers decide scope. "Not merging this" is a valid outcome and does not require a debate.
- Respect that contributors owe nothing. An abandoned PR is not a betrayal.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or sustained hostility toward anyone
- Comments demeaning a person for who they are rather than what they wrote
- Sexual attention or imagery in project spaces
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission
- Deliberately derailing discussions or reopening settled decisions to exhaust others

## Reporting

Report anything that needs attention to **mike@inite.ai**. Reports are read by the maintainer and kept confidential. You will get a reply.

If the person you need to report is the maintainer, say so plainly in the report — that is a legitimate thing to raise, and the honest answer may be that you need a venue outside this repository.

## Consequences

The maintainer may edit, hide, or delete comments; close issues and pull requests; and block accounts. Which of these applies depends on severity and on whether the behaviour continues after being named. There is no formal appeals process — this is a small project, not an institution.

## A note on scale

This document is deliberately short. A long code of conduct on a project this size signals process theatre rather than safety. If the project grows to the point where this is insufficient, it will be replaced with something more thorough, such as the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/).
